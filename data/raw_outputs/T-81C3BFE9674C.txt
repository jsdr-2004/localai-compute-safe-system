 RAM, or Random Access Memory, is like your computer's short-term memory. When you turn on your computer, the operating system and programs you're currently using are stored in the RAM so they can be quickly accessed and used by the processor.

Think of it this way: when you read a book, you don't have to go back to the library every time you want to remember a fact or a character's name. Instead, you can jot down important information on a piece of paper (or post-it notes) next to you for quick reference. This is similar to how RAM works - it stores frequently used data temporarily so the computer can access it quickly.

When you close a program or turn off your computer, all the information in the RAM gets cleared out, just like when you throw away your post-it notes. That's why you have to reload programs every time you start your computer - because the data is no longer stored in the RAM.

The more RAM a computer has, the more data it can store at once, which makes it faster and more efficient. So if you have a lot of programs running at the same time or large files open, having more RAM can make your computer run smoother and faster.
