 RAM, which stands for Random Access Memory, is like the short-term memory of a computer. When you turn on your computer, it loads data and programs from long-term storage (like a hard drive) into the RAM so that the processor can quickly access and use them. This makes your computer faster because it doesn't have to constantly go back to the hard drive to find information. Think of it like having a stack of books on your desk (hard drive) and being able to flip through them quickly (RAM). When you close a program, its data is removed from the RAM to make room for other programs. So, more RAM means your computer can handle more things at once without slowing down.
