 RAM, or Random Access Memory, is like your computer's short-term memory. When you turn on your computer, it loads the operating system and other programs you're currently using into the RAM so they can be quickly accessed and used. Think of it as a workspace where the computer does its calculations and stores data temporarily while it's being processed.

Unlike a hard drive, which is slower but holds information for longer periods, RAM loses its data when the power is turned off. So, when you close a program, it gets removed from the RAM to make space for other programs that might need to be used immediately. The more RAM your computer has, the more programs it can run at once without slowing down, as it has more workspace available.
