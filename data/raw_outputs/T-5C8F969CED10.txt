RAM, which stands for Random Access Memory, is like the temporary workspace of your computer's brain (CPU). When you're working on something or running an application, data and instructions need to be quickly accessible so that your CPU can process them. 

Think of RAM as a very fast, temporary storage area where your computer stores information it needs frequently—such as the current web page you’re looking at, open documents, or running programs. The more RAM your computer has, the better it can manage multiple tasks simultaneously without slowing down.

In contrast to hard drives and solid-state drives (HDDs/SSDs), which are much slower due to their physical nature, RAM is volatile memory, meaning it loses its data when power is turned off. That's why we need a reliable storage like an SSD or HDD for storing files long-term.

So, whenever you're using your computer and performing tasks, what you see on the screen and all the running processes are stored in RAM until they’re no longer needed, at which point they might be moved to temporary storage if necessary.
