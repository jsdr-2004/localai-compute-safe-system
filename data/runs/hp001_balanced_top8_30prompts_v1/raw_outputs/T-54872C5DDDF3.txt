To check system uptime, you can use various methods depending on your operating system. Here are some common ways to do it:

**For Windows:**

1. Open Command Prompt as an administrator.
2. Type `systeminfo` and press Enter.
3. Look for "System Up Time" in the output.

Example:
```
System Up Time: 5 days, 12 hours, 30 minutes
```

This indicates that your system has been running continuously for 5 days, 12 hours, and 30 minutes.

**For Linux (Ubuntu-based systems):**

1. Open Terminal.
2. Type `uptime` and press Enter.
3. Look for the "since" or "up" time in the output.

Example:
```
 13:45:10 up 5 days, 12:34:56.345123
```

This indicates that your system has been running continuously for 5 days, 12 hours, and approximately 34 minutes.

**For macOS (using Terminal):**

1. Open Terminal.
2. Type `uptime` and press Enter.
3. Look for the "since" or "up" time in the output.

Example:
```
13:45:10 up 5 days, 12:34:56
```

This indicates that your system has been running continuously for 5 days, 12 hours, and approximately 34 minutes.

**For Other Systems:**

You can also use the `hwclock` command on some systems to check uptime. For example:

* On some Linux distributions (like Fedora), you can type `sudo hwclock --show` and press Enter.
* On some other systems, you might need to use a different command or tool.

Keep in mind that these methods may not provide an exact uptime measurement, as they may not account for system reboots or other events. However, they should give you a general idea of how long your system has been running continuously.
