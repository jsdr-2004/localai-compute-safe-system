`df -h` is a command in the `df` (Disk Free Space) utility, which displays detailed information about the disk usage on your system.

Here's what each part of the command does:

- `df`: This stands for Disk Free Space, and it's used to display the amount of free space available on the disk(s) being monitored.
- `-h`: This option tells `df` to format the output in a human-readable way. It displays the usage in terms of percentages, which makes it easier to understand.

When you run `df -h`, here are some key points that will be displayed:

1. **Disk Name**: The name of the disk being monitored.
2. **Total Size**: The total amount of space on the disk.
3. **Used Size**: The amount of space occupied by files and other data on the disk.
4. **Available Size**: The amount of free space available on the disk.
5. **Percentage Used**: The percentage of the disk's capacity that is currently being used.

Running `df -h` will not change your system in any way, as it only displays information about the current state of the disks and does not modify their contents or configuration.

However, if you want to keep track of the disk usage over time, you can use other commands like `df -k` (which shows the disk usage in kilobytes) or `du -h` (which shows the disk usage in human-readable format).
