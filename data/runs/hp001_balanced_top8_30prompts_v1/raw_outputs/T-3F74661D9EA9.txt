 To check the system uptime in Linux, you can use the `uptime` command. Here's how to do it:

1. Open a terminal window. If you're using a graphical desktop environment like GNOME or KDE, you can usually find this by searching for "terminal" in your application launcher or by pressing `Ctrl+Alt+T`.

2. Type `uptime` and press `Enter`. The output will look something like this:

```
18:25:36 up 7 days,  9:40,  1 user,  load average: 0.02, 0.05, 0.04
```

Let me break down the output for you:

- `18:25:36` is the current time.
- `up 7 days, 9:40` means that the system has been running for 7 days and 9 hours, 40 minutes.
- `1 user` indicates there's one user currently logged in.
- `load average:` shows the system load averages for the past 1, 5, and 15 minutes. In this example:
  - `0.02` means the system was loaded by an average of 0.02 tasks per core for the last minute.
  - `0.05` means the system was loaded by an average of 0.05 tasks per core for the past 5 minutes.
  - `0.04` means the system was loaded by an average of 0.04 tasks per core for the past 15 minutes.

System load averages help you understand the CPU demand on your system at different time intervals. A lower value is generally better, as it indicates fewer processes are competing for CPU resources at a given moment. However, a consistently high load average could indicate that your system is under heavy strain and might benefit from additional resources or optimization.
