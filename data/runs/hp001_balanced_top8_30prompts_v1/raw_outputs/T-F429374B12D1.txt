For a 16GB CPU-only laptop, I would recommend a 13B (Base) model over a 3B (Boost) model. Here's why:

1. **Power efficiency**: A 13B model is designed to be more power-efficient than a 3B model. The 13B clock speed is typically around 2.5-3.0 GHz, while the 3B clock speed can range from 4.0-4.8 GHz. This means that the 13B model will consume less power and last longer on a single charge.
2. **Thermal management**: The 13B model is also designed to have better thermal management capabilities than the 3B model. This is because the 13B clock speed is typically lower, which reduces the amount of heat generated by the CPU. As a result, the laptop's thermal design will be more efficient, and it may not overheat as easily.
3. **Cost**: The cost difference between a 13B and 3B model can vary depending on the manufacturer and the specific configuration. However, in general, you can expect to pay around $100-$200 more for a 13B laptop compared to a 3B one with the same specifications.

That being said, there are some scenarios where a 3B model might be preferred:

1. **Gaming**: If you're a serious gamer, you may want to consider a 3B model with a higher clock speed and more cores. In this case, the extra power and performance might be worth the additional cost.
2. **Multitasking**: If you use your laptop for multiple tasks simultaneously (e.g., browsing, email, office work), a 3B model might provide better performance and responsiveness.

Ultimately, the choice between a 13B and 3B model depends on your specific needs and priorities. If power efficiency and thermal management are top concerns, I would recommend a 13B laptop. However, if you're a serious gamer or multitasker, a 3B model might be worth considering for its extra performance and features.
