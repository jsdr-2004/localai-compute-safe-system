Benchmark reproducibility is crucial for several reasons:

1. **Consistency**: Reproducible benchmarks ensure that the results are consistent across different environments, configurations, and systems. This helps to identify and fix issues related to hardware, software, or configuration differences.
2. **Reliability**: Reproducible benchmarks provide a reliable way to measure performance, as they eliminate variability caused by external factors like system load, network connectivity, or disk I/O. This ensures that the results accurately reflect the system's capabilities.
3. **Investigability**: Reproducible benchmarks facilitate investigation and debugging of issues. By creating a consistent baseline, researchers and developers can isolate specific problems and identify causes more easily.
4. **Comparability**: Reproducible benchmarks enable comparison between different systems, configurations, or versions of software. This helps to identify areas for improvement, optimize performance, and ensure that changes are not introducing regressions.
5. **Trust and credibility**: When benchmark results are reproducible, it builds trust in the measurement process and the system being tested. This is essential for ensuring that the results accurately reflect the system's capabilities and are not influenced by external factors.
6. **Regulatory compliance**: In some industries, such as finance or healthcare, regulatory requirements may mandate the use of reproducible benchmarks to ensure consistency and accuracy in performance measurements.

In summary, benchmark reproducibility matters because it ensures consistency, reliability, and comparability across different environments, facilitates investigation and debugging, enables comparison between systems, builds trust, and is essential for regulatory compliance.
