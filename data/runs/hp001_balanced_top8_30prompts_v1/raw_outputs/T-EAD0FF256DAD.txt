To check the system uptime, I'll assume you're referring to a computer or server. Here's how you can do it:

**Method 1: Using Online Tools**

You can use online tools to check the system uptime. Some popular options include:

* **What's My DNS**: This website allows you to check your domain's DNS records and see if they are available.
* **Pingdom**: This tool provides detailed information about your server's performance, including uptime.
* **TestMyWebsite.com**: Another option that checks your website's availability.

Enter the URL of your website or server in the search bar, and click "Check" to see if it's down.

**Method 2: Using Command Line**

If you're comfortable with the command line, you can use the `dig` (Domain Information Service) or `nslookup` commands to check the DNS records of your domain. Here's an example:

1. Open a terminal or command prompt.
2. Type `dig +short <domain_name>` and press Enter.
3. This will show you if the record is available or not.

For example, if you enter `dig +short www.example.com`, it will return `192.0.2.1`. If the response starts with "NS" followed by a number, it means that the DNS record is available.

**Result:**

If your system uptime is good, you should see an active or online status for your website or server. This indicates that it's accessible and functioning properly.

On the other hand, if you enter `dig +short <domain_name>` and get a response indicating that the record is down or not available, it means that there's an issue with your DNS records or your domain's availability.

Some possible results to look out for:

* "NS" followed by a number: This indicates that the DNS record is available.
* "SOA" (Start of Authority) record: This indicates that the server is configured correctly.
* "NS" without a number: This may indicate that there's an issue with your domain's availability or configuration.

Keep in mind that if your system uptime is low, it may not always be possible to determine the cause. A reliable uptime check should be done regularly to ensure the stability of your online presence.
