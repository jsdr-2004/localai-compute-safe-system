I'll guide you through a step-by-step process to plan a cautious disk-cleanup investigation that doesn't delete personal files.

**Before You Begin**

1. **Understand the purpose**: Determine why you want to perform this disk cleanup. Is it for:
	* Removing temporary files?
	* Erasing data from an old computer or device?
	* Preparing a system for a new user or organization?
2. **Gather necessary tools**:
	* A reliable antivirus program (optional)
	* A disk cleaning tool (e.g., CCleaner, Disk Cleanup, or Recuva)
	* A backup of your important files (in case something goes wrong)

**Step 1: Prepare the Workspace**

1. **Clear the workspace**: Remove any unnecessary items from the area where you'll be working.
2. **Set up a safe zone**: Designate an area for storing temporary files, such as a folder labeled "Temp" or "Backup."

**Step 2: Identify and Prioritize Files to Clean**

1. **Make a list of files to clean**: Start with essential documents, images, videos, and other files that you don't need immediately.
2. **Prioritize files by importance**: Consider the following factors:
	* Urgency (e.g., important documents or financial records)
	* Sensitivity (e.g., confidential information or personal data)
	* Accessibility (e.g., frequently used files)

**Step 3: Perform Disk Cleanup**

1. **Run a disk cleanup tool**: Choose a reputable disk cleaning tool, such as:
	* CCleaner (Windows) or CleanMyMac (macOS)
	* Disk Cleanup (Windows)
	* Recuva (Windows)
2. **Select the files to clean**: Follow the tool's instructions to select the files you want to remove.
3. **Review and confirm**: Review your list of files to ensure you've removed all necessary items.

**Step 4: Remove Temporary Files**

1. **Use a temporary file removal tool**: If needed, use a specialized tool like:
	* CCleaner (Windows) or CleanMyMac (macOS)
	* Disk Cleanup (Windows)
2. **Select the temporary files to remove**: Choose files that you want to delete.
3. **Confirm and proceed**: Review your list of files to ensure you've removed all necessary items.
