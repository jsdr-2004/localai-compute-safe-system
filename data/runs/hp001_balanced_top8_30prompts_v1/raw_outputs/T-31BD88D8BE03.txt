Benchmark reproducibility matters for several reasons:

1. **Verifying results**: Reproducible benchmarks ensure that the results obtained by different researchers or teams are consistent and reliable, reducing the risk of errors or biases.
2. **Comparability**: Reproducible benchmarks enable direct comparisons between different approaches, methods, or systems, facilitating the identification of best practices and areas for improvement.
3. **Advancing knowledge**: By reproducing existing results, new research can build upon established findings, accelerating progress in various fields and reducing the need to re-invent the wheel.
4. **Reducing waste**: Inefficient or incorrect benchmarks can lead to wasted resources, time, and effort. Reproducible benchmarks help minimize these risks by ensuring that experiments are well-designed and executed.
5. **Increasing transparency**: The process of creating and sharing reproducible benchmarks promotes transparency, as researchers are forced to document their methods, data, and results in detail, making it easier for others to understand and replicate their work.
6. **Fostering collaboration**: Reproducible benchmarks facilitate collaboration among researchers by providing a common framework for evaluating and comparing different approaches, leading to more effective knowledge sharing and co-operation.

By prioritizing benchmark reproducibility, we can accelerate scientific progress, reduce errors, and increase the overall efficiency of research efforts.
